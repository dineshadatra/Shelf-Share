# Competitor Research — India
## Peer-to-Peer Book Rental and Adjacent Rental Marketplaces

**Prepared:** 30 October 2026  
**Product:** ShelfShare (working product name)  
**Research method:** Public-facing websites, FAQs, terms and policies available on the research date.  
**Important limitation:** Competitor statements below are self-published claims. They are not independently audited transaction, revenue, user-count or operational-performance data. A public website or terms page does not prove that a feature is consistently available in the live app.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The Indian market contains several different models that should not be treated as direct equivalents:

1. **Peer-to-peer rental marketplaces:** GoRent and Lumo are the closest marketplace-model references. GoRent explicitly lists books and clothes; Lumo is a general physical-goods rental marketplace, not a book-specialist service.
2. **Centralised book subscription libraries:** JustBooks, mybookmark and JustBookify offer access to books through curated or centrally managed inventory and recurring/monthly plans or book cycles.
3. **Peer-to-peer book communities / lending networks:** mybook.rent, SwapSutra, Bookelphia and The Sharing Shelf describe community or owner-to-reader lending models, but their scale, current activity and operating details vary in public evidence.

**ShelfShare opportunity:** Build a focused, book-first, locality-based P2P marketplace with verified participants, clear copy-level condition records, transparent rental and deposit terms, approved public handover points, reliable return/dispute workflows and owner economics. These are not all unique features: GoRent already markets P2P book rentals with KYC and listing review, and other services already offer local lending or swaps. ShelfShare must differentiate through execution, liquidity, safety and transparency rather than feature claims alone.

## 3. Competitor notes and evidence

### 3.1 GoRent — closest direct P2P book-marketplace benchmark

**Public model**
- Books and clothes are the stated launch categories.
- Owners list physical books/clothes; renters request bookings.
- KYC and listing publication are described as human-reviewed.
- The public flow describes booking requests and owner responses, in-app chat, online payment and condition photographs.
- GoRent says it uses Razorpay, does not support cash/private UPI/pay-later, and describes deposits as held in escrow.
- Its public FAQ says deposits are released 24 hours after confirmed return unless a dispute is filed within 48 hours.
- The site describes a GoRent Shield protection product up to ₹50,000 for eligible bookings and a trust team.

**Commercial details not confirmed**
- Exact marketplace commission, gateway fee allocation, owner net earnings, revenue, active-book count and dispute rate were not established from accessible public material.
- The terms say hosts are paid after successful return, while the How It Works page describes earnings being deposited to a GoRent wallet. The legal and operational meaning of that wallet needs independent verification; ShelfShare must not copy the design without legal/payment-provider review.

**Public-content consistency caution**
- The terms say only books and clothes are supported, but some homepage testimonials refer to camera gear, MacBooks, bikes and camping gear. The homepage also displays zero counts in a trust metrics section while the blog claims a six-city launch. These inconsistencies mean the public copy should be treated as product claims, not proof of operational scale or actual catalogue availability.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Manual KYC and listing approval can be positioned as trust infrastructure, but it creates review-queue cost and delays supply activation.
- Booking requests and owner approval offer owners control, but increase time-to-confirmation and conversion risk.
- Timestamped handover/return photos and a bounded dispute filing window make condition claims more manageable.
- Clearly separate “payment provider holds funds” from “platform wallet” language; do not claim escrow unless the legal/payment arrangement genuinely supports it.

Sources: [GoRent Terms of Service](https://www.gorentweb.com/terms), [GoRent How It Works](https://www.gorentweb.com/how-it-works), [GoRent homepage](https://www.gorentweb.com/).

### 3.2 Lumo Rentals — general P2P marketplace benchmark

**Public model**
- Hosts list physical goods and renters rent locally; Lumo says it does not own or store listed items.
- Lumo identifies itself in its terms as a marketplace e-commerce entity and intermediary for user content.
- The published terms are effective 24 October 2026.
- It describes OTP login, 18+ eligibility, identity checks for renters before first booking and hosts before payout.
- A booking is confirmed when payment succeeds; the terms describe a 20-minute checkout hold.
- Rental time begins at actual physical receipt and ends when the host gets the item back.
- Host sets rent; renter pays a separately disclosed convenience fee; host commission is stated as 15–20% of rental amount only.
- Cashfree is named as payment partner.
- Lumo says it holds the deposit and uses handover/return photos in claims.
- Handover and return are confirmed in-app with a code or confirmation.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Start the rental clock at verified possession rather than payment time.
- Itemised checkout should separate rent, platform fee, delivery and deposit.
- A short payment reservation hold can prevent race conditions, but the expiry and release mechanics must be backend-controlled.
- Do not expose exact residential address in public listings.
- A general-goods marketplace provides useful trust/payment patterns, but its economics and operational risks may not transfer directly to books.

**Caution:** Lumo's published claim that it holds deposits is a description of its own arrangement, not evidence that ShelfShare may legally hold deposits on the same basis. Obtain separate payment-provider and Indian legal advice.

Sources: [Lumo Terms of Service](https://www.lumo.rentals/terms), [Lumo Bazaar](https://www.lumo.rentals/), [Lumo Deposit and Damage Policy](https://www.lumo.rentals/deposit-policy), [Lumo Host Terms](https://www.lumo.rentals/host-terms).

### 3.3 JustBooks — curated book-library benchmark

JustBooks describes itself as an online book and magazine rental service and publishes a plan customisation page. Its public page claims a catalogue of more than 10 lakh books and 2 lakh customers and identifies BAE Social Private Limited in Bengaluru.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Curated catalogue depth and membership packaging can reduce search friction.
- Subscription pricing can make repeat reading predictable.
- A centralised library can control copy condition, catalogue accuracy and fulfilment more directly than a P2P marketplace.
- These strengths come with inventory, warehousing, replenishment and logistics costs that a P2P model may avoid.

**Evidence gaps:** The accessible plan page did not expose sufficient readable policy detail to verify current deposit, verification, damage, cancellation, delivery and refund terms. The catalogue/customer numbers are operator-published claims and have not been independently verified.

Source: [JustBooks plan page](https://justbooks.in/plan).

### 3.4 mybookmark — Bengaluru subscription library benchmark

**Public model**
- Bengaluru-only service per its terms.
- 18+ and government photo ID; identity is checked in person at first delivery.
- Monthly plans published as Kids ₹249 / one book at a time / two swaps / ₹400 refundable deposit; Reader ₹549 / four books / two swaps / ₹1,000 deposit; Bookworm ₹849 / six books / four swaps / ₹1,500 deposit. A Founder plan is described as limited.
- Payment on delivery using UPI; subscription and deposit collected at first delivery.
- Doorstep delivery and pickup; swap requests mainly over WhatsApp.
- No due dates or late fees for books while subscription remains active.
- Normal wear and tear is accepted; terms state a 50% cover-price deduction for significant damage or lost books.
- Deposit refund is stated to be within seven working days after all books are returned and audited; dispute window stated as seven days.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Local service density and WhatsApp coordination can be a simple launch operating model, but they are less auditable and scalable than a fully structured workflow.
- Subscription model suits frequent readers but does not directly solve owner monetisation.
- No due dates reduce late-return friction but make inventory circulation and per-copy availability harder to guarantee.
- Explicit examples of normal wear and damage reduce disputes, though a fixed percentage of cover price may not fit every copy's actual replacement value.

Source: [mybookmark Terms & Conditions](https://www.mybookmark.in/terms).

### 3.5 JustBookify — family/children's book rental benchmark

The public FAQ describes a monthly book cycle, no deposit, no recurring membership fee and no auto-renewal. It states that the service absorbs damage rather than charging families for accidental book damage, and arranges collection at the end of the cycle.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- A zero-deposit, damage-tolerant model may be attractive for low-value children's books where customer lifetime value and repeat orders justify loss absorption.
- ShelfShare's P2P owners may not be willing to absorb damage in the same way. A standardised condition-evidence and fair compensation process is more important in a two-sided marketplace.

Source: [JustBookify FAQ](https://justbookify.in/pages/frequently-asked-questions).

### 3.6 mybook.rent — P2P book-rental positioning

The public BOOKONRENT / mybook.rent page positions itself as a peer-to-peer book rental app with title/author search and pincode-based local discovery. During this review the page showed no books found / zero visible inventory. That is a single website observation, not evidence that the company has ceased operations.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Pincode/locality discovery is a natural mental model for book lending.
- Supply density is essential: a marketplace can have a working product and still show no useful results if there are too few active listings nearby.
- Public evidence was insufficient to verify verification, deposit, handover, damage, refund or marketplace revenue policies.

Sources: [mybook.rent](https://bookonrent.in/), [alternate public domain](https://www.bookonrent.in/).

### 3.7 SwapSutra — peer book exchange/rental benchmark

SwapSutra describes a community where readers can swap, lend, rent and buy physical books. Its FAQ says users can list up to 20 books free, exact home location is not shown publicly, and a swap uses a refundable deposit plus a ₹10 fee. Its wording about rental money being paid to the owner from the deposit is not sufficiently clear to infer the exact payment architecture.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Book exchange and P2P rental can share catalogue and trust primitives, but should remain separate transaction types.
- Avoid ambiguous money-flow descriptions. Rent, deposit, platform fee and owner payout must be distinct line items and ledger events.
- Private locality display is preferable to public address exposure.

Source: [SwapSutra Support](https://www.swapsutra.in/support).

### 3.8 Bookelphia and The Sharing Shelf — community lending benchmarks

Bookelphia describes a crowdsourced book-lending network with pickup/delivery and recommendations, and says it operated in Mumbai. Its current status and detailed policies were not established.

The Sharing Shelf describes a free apartment-community sharing app piloting at Sterling Residency in Bengaluru, with no fees or ads. It is a community-sharing model rather than a commercial rental marketplace.

**Lessons for ShelfShare**
- Apartment associations, resident welfare associations, libraries and reading clubs can be supply-acquisition channels.
- Community lending can build trust with lower verification friction, but it is not equivalent to commercial rental with deposits, commission and dispute adjudication.
- Partnerships should not bypass safety, consent or public handover policies.

Sources: [Bookelphia About Us](https://www.bookelphia.com/about_us), [The Sharing Shelf](https://thesharingshelf.in/).

## 4. Business model comparison

| Model | Revenue pattern | Inventory ownership | Main cost/risk | Fit with ShelfShare |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2P commission | Percentage of rent and/or service fee | User/owner | Trust, fraud, payment fees, supply liquidity, support and disputes | High |
| Central subscription library | Monthly membership / plan fee | Platform/library operator | Inventory purchase, storage, delivery, replenishment and damaged/lost stock | Adjacent model; could be a later partner channel |
| Community lending | Usually free or low-fee | Community members | Community governance, availability and trust | Useful acquisition/partnership model |
| Hybrid marketplace | Commission plus optional membership/promotions | Both platform and users | More complex accounting, fairness and inventory management | Possible later; not needed for MVP |

## 5. Rental process and trust design comparison

| Dimension | Common observed approaches | ShelfShare product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Verification | Mobile OTP, government ID, manual review, in-person check or payout-only checks | Decide exactly when Reader and Owner must be verified; explain why; protect ID evidence |
| Listing | Owner-entered price, photos, description; some manual pre-publication review | Use copy-level actual photographs, ownership declaration, metadata separation and moderation |
| Deposit | Refundable per-booking deposit or subscription-level security deposit; some operators say they hold it | Do not implement until provider/legal custody structure is approved |
| Handover | In-app chat, delivery, doorstep pickup or local meeting | ShelfShare's approved public handover points and two-party QR/OTP sequence are a strong safety control |
| Rental clock | Some providers start at payment/day boundary; Lumo says actual possession | Start at verified collection; record exact timestamp and due timestamp |
| Returns | Photo evidence, owner confirmation, scheduled pickup | Require before/after condition evidence and return acknowledgement |
| Damage | Fixed price deduction, replacement cost, case review or no charge | Avoid automatic fixed deductions; use evidence, depreciation/repairability and appeal |
| Refund | Full/partial refund based on cancellation window; deposit after return/inspection | Publish event-by-event refund matrix before launch |
| Trust | KYC, reviews, listing moderation, in-app chat, support | Combine identity, copy evidence, location safety, transaction audit and fair resolution |

## 6. ShelfShare opportunity and product recommendations

### 6.1 Positioning
A focused neighbourhood marketplace for renting real physical books from verified local owners, with transparent pricing and safe, documented handovers.

### 6.2 Potential differentiators to validate
1. Book-first catalogue: ISBN metadata, editions, language, genre and actual copy condition.
2. Owner earnings: transparent 10% commission and clear payout statement (subject to provider and tax treatment).
3. Locality liquidity: show only currently available copies that can be collected within the chosen locality/city.
4. Safe public handovers: approved venues, no public residential addresses, two-sided QR/OTP confirmation.
5. Condition confidence: standardised front/back/spine/pages evidence, existing-damage record and return comparison.
6. Fair damage resolution: normal wear examples, proportional valuation, counter-evidence and appeal.
7. Flexible durations: 3/7/15/30 days and carefully bounded custom durations.
8. Community partnerships: colleges, apartment communities, reading clubs and local libraries.

### 6.3 Risks to avoid
- Claiming “India's first” or “only” without comprehensive market evidence.
- Copying competitor fee rates, wording, policy tables, visuals or branding.
- Treating competitor use of the word “escrow” as proof that the structure is legally suitable for ShelfShare.
- Launching across large geographies before local copy density and handover operations exist.
- Launching community/chat before moderation and safety staffing are ready.
- Publishing ratings/reliability signals without clear criteria, review rights and anti-manipulation controls.

## 7. Research limitations and next validation steps

**Not independently established in this desk research**
- Actual transaction volumes, GMV, revenue, contribution margin, repeat rental rates or market share for any competitor.
- Actual customer satisfaction or dispute outcomes.
- Whether every published competitor feature is live and consistently available in production apps.
- Current JustBooks deposit/refund policy.
- Full mybook.rent and SwapSutra payment/verification workflows.
- Competitor legal/payment provider arrangements beyond what their own public terms state.

**Recommended primary research**
1. Install and test GoRent and Lumo from both renter and host perspectives; capture current checkout, cancellation and deposit screens.
2. Call JustBooks and mybookmark to validate plans, current delivery coverage, fees and deposit handling.
3. Attempt a test search by locality on mybook.rent and SwapSutra; confirm active inventory and booking process.
4. Interview at least 15 potential readers and 15 potential owners across two pilot localities per city.
5. Test willingness to pay for 3/7/15/30-day rentals, deposits, pickup-point travel and damage protection.
6. Conduct a small concierge pilot before committing to automated payment/deposit handling.
